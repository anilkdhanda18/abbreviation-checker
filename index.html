Proofing report

This is a heuristic aid, not a substitute for a careful read. Detection of abbreviations and definitions is pattern-based and can still miss unusual cases. All-caps paper titles and other excluded front matter are not treated as abbreviation sources. Definitions are recognised when the acronym sits beside its parenthetical (either order), including phrasings like "hereafter" or "abbreviated as". Requirements vary by journal, so always confirm against your target journal's author guidelines.

What this checks
  1. Define at first use. Every non-standard abbreviation should be spelled out in full the first time it appears, with the short form in parentheses.
  2. Do not use before defining. The full form must appear at or before the first use, not later.
  3. Earn the abbreviation. Defined terms that are then used only once or twice are flagged.
  4. Titles and headings excluded. The paper title and section/subsection headings, including numbered, ALL-CAPS, and Title Case headings, are not used for abbreviation-definition checks.
  5. Front matter excluded. Author/affiliation blocks, correspondence details, and Keywords/Index terms are not used for abbreviation-definition checks.
  6. Plurals without apostrophes. MFCs, not MFC's.
  7. Do not begin a sentence with an abbreviation.
  8. Define once. Repeated redefinition is flagged; a separate definition in the abstract versus the main text is acceptable.
  9. List of abbreviations. Suggested when many abbreviations are present and no such section is found.
  10. References are skipped by default so citations and journal acronyms do not cause false flags.
  11. Structural content is skipped. Word tables, figure/table captions, displayed equations, headers/footers, footnotes/endnotes, and References are excluded from abbreviation-definition checks.
  12. Table-note definitions are accepted. Reverse forms such as CE: Coulombic efficiency, CE = Coulombic efficiency, and equivalent list-style definitions are recognised. If the long form already occurred earlier in body prose, the earlier prose occurrence is reported so the abbreviation can be introduced there.

Reference report

The reference ledger reports whether key bibliographic components are detected in each entry. Field applicability is inferred from the reference type and the dominant structure of the reference list. Detection is heuristic, so ambiguous entries should still be checked manually.

What this checks
  1. Reference ledger. Each entry is assessed for author, year, title, source, pages or article number, volume, and DOI.
  2. Style-aware applicability. Fields intentionally omitted by the dominant reference-list convention are shown as n/a rather than as errors.
  3. Reference type. Journal articles and non-journal sources are distinguished so inapplicable journal fields are not treated as required.
  4. Ledger-only report. The References tab displays the field-status ledger without separate warning or suggestion sections.

Number, pH & precision report

How to read this report: findings are grouped by rule. The checker distinguishes ordinary number style, pH reporting, value ± variation consistency, ranges, p-values, and comparable precision patterns. Precision findings are intended for genuinely comparable data, not unrelated quantities or predefined thresholds.

This combines numeral usage, pH reporting, and scientific numeric-precision checks. References and headings are excluded. Word tables are checked for precision patterns only within genuinely comparable measurement columns. Precision is assessed only within comparable numeric groups, including numerical series, ranges, uncertainties, percentages, p-values, and scientific notation. The checker never invents measurement resolution or silently adds or removes significant digits; uncertain cases are flagged for author review.

What the number, pH and precision check covers
  1. Numerals vs words. Whole numbers zero to nine used as ordinary counts in prose are suggested to be spelled out; measurements, units, statistics, ratios, dates, and labels stay as numerals.
  2. pH reporting. Measured, adjusted, or target pH values should carry at least one decimal place, and directly comparable pH measurements are checked for mixed precision.
  3. Leading zeros. Decimals below one should be written as 0.5, not .5.
  4. Sentence start. Beginning a sentence with a numeral is flagged so the sentence can be restructured.
  5. Comparable precision. Decimal places or significant figures are compared only within scientifically plausible groups, not across unrelated quantities.
  6. Table-column precision. Checks consistent precision within the same parameter column.
  7. Uncertainty and ranges. A reported value and its ± variation/uncertainty should end at the same decimal place; the checker does not impose a separate significant-digit limit on the variation. Range endpoints are also checked for compatible precision.
  8. Statistics. p-values, percentages, confidence-style numerical series, and scientific notation receive conservative consistency checks.
  9. No false precision. The checker does not automatically add trailing zeros, remove justified digits, or alter standards and literature values. It flags uncertain precision for author review.

Units & notation report

Purpose: this module checks internal consistency of repeated units and their associated numerical precision. It does not force SI notation and does not compare unrelated units. Affiliations and references are excluded.

This check focuses on internal unit consistency. References and affiliations are excluded. The checker looks for meaningful inconsistencies in repeated unit notation or numerical precision; harmless exponent typography such as m2, m^2, and m² is treated as equivalent. It does not require SI notation and does not compare unrelated units.

What the units and notation check covers
  1. Unit recognition. Common scientific and environmental-engineering unit expressions are recognized as measurement units rather than abbreviations.
  2. Same-unit format. Equivalent forms such as mg/L, mg L⁻¹, and mg·L⁻¹ are recognized as the same unit; mixed use is reported only as an internal consistency issue.
  3. Same-unit precision. Repeated values for the same parameter and same unit are checked for inconsistent numerical precision, for example 10 mg/L versus 10.0 mg/L.
  4. Convention consistency. A notation is not rejected merely because it is non-SI. If one format is used consistently, it is accepted; mixed formatting of the same unit is reported.
  5. Analyte basis. Expressions such as mg N/L, mg NO3-N/L, and mg COD/L retain their analytical basis rather than being merged solely by physical dimension.
  6. Word tables. A header such as BOD (mg/L) supplies unit context to the numeric cells below it. Precision is compared only within the same parameter and unit.
  7. No automatic data edits. The checker may compare conversion factors internally but does not rewrite numerical results or invent measurement precision.

English, grammar & style report

How to read this report: the checker combines manuscript-specific English consistency with conservative grammar and style checks. It infers the dominant US/British convention, checks third-person and tense consistency, grammar/spelling, punctuation, clarity/conciseness, terminology consistency, readability, and the 100–300 word paragraph rule. References and affiliations are excluded. The Abstract is additionally excluded from paragraph-length checking. Context-sensitive or uncertain cases are treated as review items rather than automatic corrections.

This module combines manuscript-specific English consistency with conservative grammar and scientific-style checks. References and affiliations are excluded. Suggestions are intended for author review and are not automatic edits.

What the English consistency check covers
  1. Dominant convention first. The checker counts diagnostic US and British spelling markers across the manuscript and establishes the majority convention only when there is sufficient evidence.
  2. Contradictions only. Once a dominant convention is established, only minority spellings are flagged. The report shows the conflicting word and its manuscript context.
  3. References and affiliations excluded. Author affiliations, addresses, and the complete reference list do not influence the inferred language convention.
  4. Conservative vocabulary. Ambiguous pairs such as practice/practise, license/licence, and program/programme are not used automatically because usage can depend on grammatical role or meaning.
  5. Strict third-person check. Unambiguous first-person forms such as I, we, our, my, and us, and second-person forms such as you and your, are flagged in manuscript prose. Every potentially personal form is interpreted from context before being flagged. This prevents technical uses such as Type I, a mathematical/current symbol I, US for United States, unit-like us, acronym-like tokens, and mining-related mine from being confused with first- or second-person language. This is implemented as a strict manuscript-style rule rather than a universal rule of scientific English.
  6. Scientific tense check. Tense is evaluated in context. Tense review is allowed for general truths, definitions, established properties, ongoing conditions, continuing limitations, and interpretations that remain valid. Tense review is flagged mainly when a sentence clearly narrates a completed manuscript-specific experimental action or observation that should normally be in past tense.
  7. Paragraph length. Main-text prose paragraphs should contain 100–300 words. Paragraphs below 100 or above 300 words are flagged. The Abstract and References are excluded, as are obvious headings, captions, equations, and table-like structural lines.
  8. Grammar and spelling. Checks high-confidence issues such as common scientific typos, duplicated words, article misuse, clear subject–verb disagreement, redundant comparative forms, and selected commonly confused constructions.
  9. Punctuation and mechanics. Checks repeated punctuation, spaces before punctuation, missing spaces after commas/semicolons/colons, and unmatched brackets in prose.
  10. Clarity and conciseness. Flags selected wordy phrases, redundant expressions, informal contractions/phrases, sentences longer than 30 words, repeated sentence openings, overused transition words, vague sentence-opening This, and limited high-confidence parallel-structure problems.
  11. Terminology consistency. Detects mixed hyphenation, open/closed variants such as wastewater/waste water, and repeated technical terms with inconsistent internal capitalization.
  12. Readability. Passive constructions are reported as informational review items, not automatic errors. The report also gives sentence-length indicators rather than treating readability as a measure of scientific quality.
  13. No forced rewriting. The checker does not change the manuscript. It reports the dominant convention, flags first/second-person usage, and provides a concise revision suggestion.

Figures, tables & equations report

This check uses the structure of the uploaded Word document to distinguish captions/items from ordinary cross-references. Figures, tables, equations, and supplementary items are validated as independent sequences. Placement is flagged only when the Word structure provides sufficient evidence; otherwise the report states that placement could not be verified reliably.

What the figure, table and equation check covers
  1. Independent sequences. Figures, tables, and numbered equations are checked separately, including supplementary S-numbered items.
  2. Numbering. Missing, duplicate, skipped, mixed, and out-of-order identifiers are flagged where the numbering scheme is clear.
  3. Cross-references. Grouped citations such as Figs. 2–5, Tables 1 and 2, and Eqs. (3)–(5) are expanded and checked against existing items.
  4. Citation before appearance. Figures and tables should be cited in manuscript text before their first physical appearance. Caption text does not count as the prior citation.
  5. Equations. Only structurally numbered displayed equations are included. Citation-before-equation placement is reported conservatively because journal requirements vary.
  6. Multipanel figures. Panel references are treated as components of the parent figure; obvious panel-letter gaps or duplicates in captions are flagged.
