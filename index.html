Proofing report

This is a heuristic aid, not a substitute for a careful read. Detection of acronyms, definitions, and section boundaries is pattern-based and can still miss cases or over-flag chemical formulae and all-caps words. Definitions are recognised when the acronym sits beside its parenthetical (either order), including phrasings like "hereafter" or "abbreviated as". Requirements vary by journal, so always confirm against your target journal's author guidelines.

What this checks
  1. Define at first use. Every non-standard abbreviation should be spelled out in full the first time it appears, with the short form in parentheses.
  2. Do not use before defining. The full form must appear at or before the first use, not later.
  3. Earn the abbreviation. Defined terms that are then used only once or twice are flagged.
  4. No abbreviations in the title unless universally recognised (checked when a title is provided).
  5. Plurals without apostrophes. MFCs, not MFC's.
  6. Do not begin a sentence with an abbreviation.
  7. Define once. Repeated redefinition is flagged; a separate definition in the abstract versus the main text is acceptable.
  8. Units are not pluralised (kg, not kgs).
  9. List of abbreviations. Suggested when many abbreviations are present and no such section is found.
  10. References are skipped by default so citations and journal acronyms do not cause false flags.
  11. Tables are skipped by default. Word tables are removed directly, and numeric data rows with their header line are filtered from PDF and pasted text.

Reference report

This checks that each reference carries the components needed for formatting (authors, year, title area, source, volume or issue, pages, DOI) and that in-text citations and the list agree. It is heuristic and style-agnostic: it does not reformat references into a specific journal style, and title and source names cannot always be isolated from plain text. For exact reformatting, a reference manager such as Zotero is the right tool.

What this checks
  1. Completeness. Each entry is checked for authors, publication year, a title area, source, volume or issue, page range, and DOI.
  2. Style detection. The dominant style family is identified (numbered such as Vancouver or IEEE, or author-year such as APA or Harvard).
  3. In-text vs list. Every numbered citation in the text has a matching entry, and every listed reference is actually cited.
  4. Consistency. Duplicate references, gaps or repeats in the numbering, and DOIs missing from only some entries are flagged.

Number & pH report

This checks numeral usage and pH reporting in the manuscript body (references, tables, and headings are excluded). It flags pH values reported without a decimal place, decimals missing a leading zero, small counts in prose that are usually spelled out, and sentences that begin with a numeral. It is context-aware and conservative: measurements, units, statistics, identifiers, dates, and chemical or scientific numbers are left as numerals. Precision is never invented; integer pH values are flagged for you to verify against the measurement, not silently changed.

What the number check covers
  1. Numerals vs words. Whole numbers zero to nine used as ordinary counts in prose are suggested to be spelled out; measurements, units, statistics, ratios, dates, and labels stay as numerals.
  2. pH precision. Measured, adjusted, or target pH values should carry at least one decimal place; integer pH values and integer pH ranges or thresholds are flagged for verification.
  3. Leading zeros. Decimals below one should be written as 0.5, not .5.
  4. Sentence start. Beginning a sentence with a numeral is flagged so the sentence can be restructured.
  5. Not invented precision. Values are flagged for author verification rather than changed, and authoritative or conceptual pH values are respected.
